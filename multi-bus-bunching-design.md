Status: the simulation refresh, grouped HOLD and grouped DWELL workflows have passed running-stack verification.

## Completed

- Incident-specific simulation group resolution, multi-bus controller outcomes (including reasoned no-instruction decisions), captain delivery and acknowledgement, and simulation skip-stop instructions have been implemented. The planning forms support larger groups; simulation runs currently allow up to 20 buses.
- Disruption now retains its latest evaluated bunching state, source snapshot, affected observation IDs and freshness deadline. The PostgreSQL-backed simulation test covers replacement by newer evidence, insufficient evidence and out-of-order snapshots.
- An ingestion-peer-only disruption endpoint exposes that evaluation for an active simulation episode, including whether its source snapshot is fresh. It reports missing evidence as unavailable.
- Ingestion resolves the current detector observations to ordered, unique bus identities and exposes them to the planning peer. It rejects stale or insufficient evidence, mismatched run/route/stop, missing or ambiguous identities, and a detector snapshot that is older than the latest successful replay snapshot.
- The current-group response includes prediction gaps between successive affected buses, calculated from the same source snapshot. These are gaps between the listed buses; other, non-affected buses may be between them.
- Planning can persist a recommendation revision linked to its predecessor and source snapshot. Supersession is transactional, permits only one successor, retains the previous record, removes it from the pending queue, and serializes against decisions on that predecessor. A decision already recorded prevents supersession.
- Before issuing a new simulation bunching decision, planning checks the current resolved group, route and freshness against the reviewed buses. Changed, expired or unavailable evidence causes the transaction to roll back. Existing issued-instruction replays retain their existing behavior.
- The Planning page can refresh a pending recommendation using the latest ordered group and prediction gaps. It displays those gaps separately from confirmation evidence and requires a new outcome for each bus. Issuance checks the revision’s exact source snapshot. Every grouped HOLD uses its bus’s own preceding prediction gap and fresh route progress. Planning reports the bus on an assignment or delivery failure and rolls back the whole decision. Instruction history shows acknowledgement deadlines and missed acknowledgements.
- Grouped DWELL requires the bus to be in its synthetic boarding window at the reviewed stop and route occurrence. The window starts at the estimated arrival and ends one minute later or at the source freshness deadline, whichever comes first. Planning caps the adjustment at the smaller of the bus’s preceding headway deficit and the 75-second simulation dwell limit. Through Nginx, a boarding bus received DWELL while the next bus received HOLD, and both captains acknowledged their instructions.

## Policy and implementation details

- Scope is incident-level review for all affected buses, including groups larger than three. Derive membership and bus order from incident observations, not the full service fleet; expose unresolved identity or ordering rather than guessing.
- A controller reviews an outcome for every affected bus. An outcome may be an instruction or no instruction needed with a recorded reason. Revalidate membership and relevant state before issuing; require a renewed review when they change.
- Display the observed timing gap for each adjacent bus pair, including missing or uncertain values, and support every affected bus in the recommendation detail and controller override forms.
- In simulation, retain the existing hold and dwell policy as the starting point, validating actions for each bus. Support controller-selected skip-stop instructions for simulation, with a specific, unambiguous upcoming stop and captain assignment; do not assume that simulated stop eligibility proves real-world safety.
- Live instruction issuance remains out of scope and keep-in-view. Existing simulation limits are not approved live operating limits. Live skip-stop eligibility and passenger requirements require operational rules and data before live issuance.
- Track individual instruction delivery and acknowledgement and surface assignment or delivery failures. Preserve transactional consistency and retry behavior.
- Existing API contracts, storage limits, frontend types, simulation behavior, and tests must be updated together. No implementation should be considered complete without automated and running-application verification.
- Confirmed alerts can include observations from multiple snapshots. Resolve and deduplicate by bus identity; retain pair ordering and gaps from the relevant snapshot rather than assuming one observation equals one bus.
- Planning mitigation contexts, controller decisions and early suggestions allow up to 100 distinct affected buses as an internal bound. The simulation run itself supports up to 20 buses.
- Simulation run alert enrichment resolves affected observations from the confirmed snapshot, orders their vehicles by the saved arrival estimates and returns no affected-vehicle list when required evidence or identities cannot be resolved uniquely. Planning reads that group through a planning-peer-only endpoint and rejects missing or mismatched groups without substituting fleet or fixture buses. The live-alert response still deduplicates by bus ID and retains all observation IDs; missing-identity diagnostics are not yet exposed to controllers.
- Disruption snapshot validation accepts up to 100 arrival slots, and the detector evaluates each adjacent gap; the simulation configuration and ingestion database still restrict what can reach that detector.
- Simulation replay, the start-run API, and the form support 2 to 20 buses, matching the existing demo-run database bound. Migration V027 allows 20 simulation arrival slots while retaining the 3-slot observation-only limit. The ingestion OpenAPI contract is generated from the application; the database-backed four-bus replay check covers the migration.
- Recommendation gap display uses the recorded alert headways to include adjacent prediction slots beyond the number of bus references currently supplied by planning. Slot numbers remain observation order, not verified vehicle identities.
- Route-strip gap calculations inspect up to 100 saved prediction slots, retaining the existing three-slot alert table. These slots are not stable vehicle identities.
- Planning decision and early-suggestion storage accept up to 100 per-bus entries, matching the mitigation context bound. Multi-bus decisions require one instruction or reasoned no-instruction outcome per affected bus. Approve is available for a group only when the recommendation stores a suggested outcome for every affected bus; it issues exactly those displayed outcomes, records the decision as APPROVED with no overrides, and runs the same revalidation as a controller override. Without complete suggestions, the controller must enter each outcome.
- Before a new decision, planning checks the original confirmed group, verifies that each bus resolves in the active fleet, and compares it with ingestion's latest resolved group in arrival order. The evidence is a prediction snapshot, not physical position telemetry; it can also change after the last cross-service check. Grouped holds use each bus’s preceding prediction gap.
- Disruption retains the latest evaluated bunching state, source snapshot, affected observation IDs and freshness deadline per route/stop/direction. Ingestion resolves that evidence for planning's issuance check.
- Skip-stop overrides are simulation-only and target the recommendation control stop. They require a unique stop occurrence at or beyond the bus's predicted stop, with a fresh prediction whose estimated arrival is still in the future. The prediction's freshness window is supplied by ingestion. Instructions are issued for captain acknowledgement; synthetic replay does not model passenger eligibility or change vehicle movement in response to instructions.
